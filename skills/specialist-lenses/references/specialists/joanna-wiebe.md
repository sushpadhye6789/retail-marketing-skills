# Joanna Wiebe

**Lens:** Copy that converts isn't written from imagination or best-practice templates — it's mined from the customer's own words, then structured and edited with the same rigor as a scientific test.

## Public framework

Wiebe coined the term **conversion copywriting** and built Copyhackers around a repeatable process: start with **research and discovery** — mining reviews, support tickets, surveys, and interviews for the customer's actual language about their problem — since this is the largest part of the work and everything else depends on it. Organize the resulting copy using a message structure (she favors **PAS** — Problem, Agitate, Solve — when there's a clear pain point, or **AIDA** — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action — when there isn't). Finish with **The Seven Sweeps**, a structured multi-pass editing process that tightens the draft before it goes into A/B testing.

## Retail application

This is a direct, high-fit extension of `copywriting` and `copy-editing`: the "mine the customer's own words first" discipline is exactly what those skills should be doing before drafting product-page or campaign copy, and the Seven Sweeps gives `copy-editing` a concrete, repeatable editing process rather than a general "make it better" instruction. For retail specifically, review mining (a large, retail-native data source most brands already have and under-use) is often the single highest-leverage version of her "research first" discipline.

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Product-page and landing-page copy specifically, where conversion is the direct, measurable goal; a structured editing pass before anything ships to a split test.
**Blind spots:** Built for direct-response, conversion-focused copy — the PAS/AIDA structures are less suited to brand-building or awareness content where the goal isn't an immediate action, and applying conversion-copy discipline to top-of-funnel brand storytelling can flatten tone that should stay more expansive.
