# Retail Funnel Framework — Primer for Plan Sequencing

**Awareness → Consideration → Conversion → Loyalty** is the spine of every plan produced by this skill. It replaced AARRR (Dave McClure's "pirate metrics" — Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue) because AARRR is a SaaS/startup growth framework: "Activation" means a first valued *product session* (onboarding, trial, first key action), which doesn't describe how a retail purchase actually happens. This doc is the primer + the decision rules for when each stage gets prioritized.

## The four stages

| Stage | Question | Common metrics |
|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | How do strangers become aware of us? | Visits, impressions, App Store / storefront visits, social reach, CAC by channel |
| **Consideration** | Once aware, do they research and compare us seriously? | Product-page views, session depth, reviews read, cart adds without purchase, comparison-page traffic, wishlist/save rate |
| **Conversion** | Do they complete the purchase — and is that first order healthy? | Checkout completion rate, cart abandonment rate, first-order AOV, gross margin per order, CAC payback on that order |
| **Loyalty** | Do they come back, deepen the relationship, and bring others? | Repeat-purchase rate, 90/180/365-day retention, LTV, referral/advocacy rate, subscription/replenishment adoption |

> **Revenue is not a fifth stage.** AARRR's Revenue bucket (pricing, ARPU, LTV, expansion) splits across two points instead: **initial-purchase economics live in Conversion** (AOV, margin, CAC payback on the first order), and **repeat-purchase economics live in Loyalty** (LTV, repeat AOV, subscription/replenishment revenue, referral-driven revenue). This mirrors how Brand and Content are cross-cutting rather than their own stage (below) — Revenue is a cross-cutting *economic lens* applied at two specific points in the funnel, not a bucket of its own.
>
> **Referral is not a fifth stage either.** It's a named subsection *inside* Loyalty ("Referral & Advocacy") — a loyal customer becoming an advocate is a downstream behavior of loyalty, not a separate funnel stage a stranger passes through.

> **Consideration boundary rule.** Awareness ends and Consideration begins the moment a stranger does something that signals active interest rather than passive exposure — reading a product page, comparing options, saving an item, reading reviews. Consideration ends and Conversion begins the moment they start the transaction (add to cart *with checkout intent*, open the checkout flow, submit a trial-to-paid decision). Apply this rule consistently across all docs and the plan template.

## Why this framework for plan sequencing

Three reasons — the same three AARRR was chosen for, now anchored in how a retail purchase actually unfolds.

**1. Funnel-stage tagging forces prioritization.** Without a funnel lens, marketing plans become channel-organized ("here's the SEO plan, here's the social plan, here's the paid plan"). Channels can address multiple stages; tagging by stage instead asks the more useful question: *what stage of the funnel is the binding constraint right now?*

**2. Fix the leak before pouring water in.** The Consideration/Conversion question ("does the funnel convert at acceptable rates given exposure?") is usually higher leverage than the Awareness question ("how do we get more exposure?"). Stage sequencing surfaces this naturally.

**3. The Loyalty conversation is honest.** Most marketing plans bury repeat-purchase economics and referral under vague "growth" language and treat advocacy as wishful thinking. Naming Loyalty as its own stage — with Referral & Advocacy and repeat-purchase economics as explicit subsections — forces both into the open.

## Brand and content — not a stage, cross-cutting

A common mistake: making "Brand" or "Content" a fifth bucket. They're not — they serve every stage, the same way Revenue is a lens rather than a stage.

- **Brand voice** governs every piece of copy across every stage
- **Content** feeds Awareness (SEO, social), Consideration (comparison pages, reviews, buying guides, product detail), Conversion (checkout trust signals, cart-recovery copy), Loyalty (lifecycle email, ambassador talking points, replenishment reminders)

In the plan, brand/content shows up as the strategic frame (Section 2) and cross-cutting in Section 10's ops stack — never as its own funnel section.

## Diagnosing the binding constraint — which stage is highest leverage?

For every client, one or two stages will be the binding constraint. The plan sequences moves there first.

**Decision rules:**

### If you don't have any customers → start with Awareness
- Pre-launch / new-brand / new-location stage
- No funnel data exists
- Leverage = building the first cohort of aware prospects

### If you have traffic but it doesn't turn into carts or considered visits → start with Consideration
- Traffic arrives but bounces without engaging product pages
- No comparison, review, or research content exists to hold attention
- Session depth is shallow; product pages have high exit rates
- Leverage = giving a stranger a reason to seriously evaluate the brand before they leave

### If people consider but don't buy → start with Conversion
- Cart abandonment is high
- Checkout has friction (forced account creation, hidden shipping costs, weak trust signals)
- First-order economics are murky (AOV, margin, CAC payback unknown or unhealthy)
- Leverage = removing friction between "in the cart" and "money changes hands"

### If conversion works but customers don't return → start with Loyalty
- First-purchase conversion is healthy but repeat-purchase rate is low
- No lifecycle infrastructure exists (post-purchase, replenishment, win-back)
- No referral/advocacy mechanism captures word-of-mouth that's already happening
- Leverage = lifecycle, deepening the relationship, and turning repeat customers into advocates

### If everything works at small scale → start with Awareness (scaling)
- Funnel is healthy end to end
- Question is just "more of it"
- This is the "post-fit" scaling problem

## Stage-by-stage strategic patterns

### Awareness

**The diagnostic question:** Where is the gap between category-level awareness and current funnel volume? What channels are saturated by competitors vs. open?

**Common Awareness moves:**
- SEO content strategy (organic compounding)
- Founder-led / brand-led channels (Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn for B2B/wholesale)
- Paid acquisition (when budget unlocks)
- Marketplace / storefront listing optimization (Amazon, App Store/Play Store for a hybrid app, Shopify collection pages)
- PR and credibility-anchor amplification
- Events (in-store, pop-up, trade show, conference)
- Partnerships (newsletter swaps, co-marketing, wholesale/dealer partners)
- B2B/wholesale sales support (line sheets, trade directories, vertical content)

**Sequencing principle:** Build the organic compound first (SEO + founder-led + content + PR amplification + ambassadors). Only layer paid on top of a working organic baseline. Premature paid amplifies what's broken downstream.

### Consideration

**The diagnostic question:** Once someone is aware, what do they need to see, read, or compare before they trust this brand enough to buy? What's stopping a browser from becoming a considered shopper?

**Common Consideration moves:**
- Product-page depth and clarity (specs, sizing, materials, use cases, high-quality imagery)
- Reviews and social proof surfaced where the decision is being made (product page, not buried on a separate tab)
- Comparison / "vs. competitor" content and buying guides
- Category and credibility content (a science page, a sourcing story, a certification callout) that a skeptical shopper reads before adding to cart
- Wishlist / save-for-later / cart-without-checkout capture, with light retargeting (not aggressive)
- Marketplace or App Store listing copy and screenshots — the pre-download research surface, not the post-download experience

**Sequencing principle:** Give the shopper what they need to convince themselves, in their own research process — don't rush them to checkout before they've resolved their own doubts. Everything that closes a real, reasonable doubt (not everything that adds friction) belongs here.

### Conversion

**The diagnostic question:** Where does an interested shopper decide *not* to complete the purchase, and what would remove that friction? Is the resulting first order actually healthy once it happens?

**Common Conversion moves:**
- Checkout friction removal (guest checkout, fewer form fields, saved payment methods)
- Cart-abandonment recovery (email/SMS, retargeting, incentive only if margin supports it)
- Trust signals at the point of purchase (secure-checkout badges, clear returns/shipping policy, visible guarantees)
- Free-trial-to-paid or subscription sign-up friction (for a hybrid subscription/hardware business — this is the retail-native "does the trial convert" question)
- First-order pricing structure (intro offers, bundle framing) — evaluated against margin, not just conversion rate
- Initial unit-economics audit: AOV, gross margin per order, blended CAC, and CAC payback period on that first transaction (see `ads/references/payback-period.md`)

**Sequencing principle:** Fix the highest-volume leak in the checkout/purchase path first. A cheap fix to a high-traffic step (e.g., removing forced account creation) usually outperforms a large fix to a low-traffic step. Once the funnel converts acceptably, audit whether what converts is actually profitable — a "working" checkout that loses money per order isn't a win.

### Loyalty

**The diagnostic question:** Why do customers not come back? What's the second reason to purchase, after the first one? Is word-of-mouth happening without a structure to capture it?

**Common Loyalty moves:**
- Lifecycle email/SMS flows: post-purchase, replenishment reminder, lapsed-customer re-engagement, win-back
- Loyalty / rewards / subscribe-and-save programs
- Churn/lapse reconciliation (often metric definitions don't match across surfaces)
- Support as marketing (high-touch moments that drive stories and repeat trust)
- Community + repeat-buyer networks

**Referral & Advocacy (subsection of Loyalty):**
- Ambassador / affiliate program (start with inbound interest, not cold recruitment)
- Share-after-purchase moments built into the post-purchase experience
- Founder amplification (founder as referrer-zero)
- Gifting flows (consumer / hardware)
- Two-sided referral rewards (reward both referrer and referred)

**Repeat-purchase economics (subsection of Loyalty):**
- LTV by cohort and acquisition source
- Repeat AOV vs. first-order AOV (does basket size grow on repeat visits?)
- Subscription/replenishment revenue as a share of total revenue
- Referral-driven revenue and its blended CAC (referred customers are usually cheaper to acquire — quantify it)

**Sequencing principle:** Ship lifecycle flows in the order their content is most stable. Post-purchase flows that don't reference in-flux product/UI details ship first. Anything referencing a screen or feature that might change ships last. Win-back is a recurring campaign, not a one-time flow. Referral & Advocacy launches with whoever is already raising their hand — don't wait for a "complete program."

## How to assign a move to a stage

Some moves clearly belong to one stage. Others span. The rule:

**Assign to the stage where the move's primary measurable impact lands.**

Examples:
- "Rewrite the App Store/marketplace listing in brand voice" — spans Awareness (organic discovery) and Consideration (the research surface a prospect reads before downloading/buying). Primary impact = Consideration (does the listing convince them to try). Assign to Consideration, mention the Awareness crossover.
- "Product-page rewrite with SEO keyword targeting" — spans Awareness (organic search) and Conversion (page-level conversion rate). Primary impact = Conversion (the sale). Assign to Conversion, mention the Awareness crossover.
- "Founder's LinkedIn cadence" — Awareness (top of funnel for the brand).
- "Post-purchase lifecycle flow (hardware buyer)" — Loyalty (deepens engagement toward repeat purchase) with crossover to Conversion (if it also drives an upsell/subscription attach at time of purchase).

When in doubt: where would removing this move hurt the most? Assign there.

## When the stage breakdown isn't equal

For most clients, the plan won't have equal volume across stages. That's fine — and worth surfacing as a diagnostic.

- **Heavy Awareness section** = client has product-market fit but top-of-funnel is the bottleneck. Common for early-stage brands with strong repeat-purchase metrics.
- **Heavy Consideration section** = client has traffic but nothing convinces the browser to trust the brand. Often thin product pages, no reviews, no comparison content.
- **Heavy Conversion section** = client has interested shoppers but checkout leaks them. Often a technical/UX fix problem more than a marketing-message problem.
- **Heavy Loyalty section** = client has a repeat-purchase or advocacy problem. Often mid-stage brands that scaled acquisition past their lifecycle infrastructure.

If a plan ends up evenly distributed across all four stages, the diagnostic was probably weak — re-examine the funnel state intake to find where the binding constraint is.

## A note on the order of presentation

Always present the funnel in order (Awareness → Consideration → Conversion → Loyalty) regardless of priority order.

This is for the reader's mental model. Founders and owners expect the funnel to flow top-to-bottom. If Loyalty is the most-leveraged stage but you lead with Loyalty, the reader has to context-switch.

To signal priority, use the executive summary (Section 1) — name the biggest bets there. The stage-by-stage breakdown then walks the funnel in order, with the most leverage-positive section being the longest and most-detailed.
