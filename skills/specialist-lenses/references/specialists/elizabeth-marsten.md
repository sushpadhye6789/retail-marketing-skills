# Elizabeth Marsten

**Lens:** Retail media decisions should be made at the portfolio level, with measurement built in from the start — not bolted on after the fact, and not chasing every new retailer/format individually.

## Public framework

Marsten is VP of Innovation & Growth, Commerce Media at **Tinuiti**, and a regular contributor to AdWeek on retail media. Her public commentary centers on treating retail media as a portfolio-management problem: choosing which retail media networks (Amazon, Walmart Connect, Target Roundel, Instacart, and others) to invest in based on audience and distribution fit, building an analytics stack that can reconcile performance *across* networks rather than judging each in isolation, using always-on incrementality testing rather than one-off tests, and reserving deliberate room for experimentation with new formats instead of chasing every new placement type as it launches.

## Retail application

This directly extends this repo's `retail-media` skill's existing coverage with the cross-network portfolio lens: a retailer running ads on both Amazon and Walmart Connect needs one measurement framework that can compare the two honestly (attribution methods differ by platform), not two separate scorecards that can't be reconciled. It also reinforces `attribution`'s incrementality-checkpoint discipline specifically for retail media — a channel where it's especially easy to credit a sale to a retail-media placement that would have converted anyway (the shopper was already searching for the product on that retailer's site).

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Deciding budget allocation across multiple retail media networks rather than optimizing one in isolation; building a reconciled measurement stack before scaling spend.
**Blind spots:** Perspective comes from an agency serving mid-to-large advertisers across many retailers — a single-retailer or early-stage retail-media presence needs a simpler starting framework before "portfolio-level" thinking is the right lens to apply.
