# Julia McCoy

**Lens:** Raw AI-generated content reads as generic and untrustworthy no matter how fluent it sounds — the value only shows up after deliberate human editing, and the human's own experience is the one ingredient AI structurally cannot supply.

## Public framework

McCoy (President at Content at Scale) developed the **CRAFT framework** for editing AI-generated content: **Cut** the fluff AI tends to repeat, **Review** for accuracy and flow, **Add** genuine expertise and examples the AI couldn't have known, **Fact-check** every claim, and **Trust-build** by weaving in real, personal experience and credibility signals. Her stated position — repeated across her public content — is that every CRAFT step except the last is optional depending on the content, but Trust-build is not: AI can approximate expertise-sounding prose, but it cannot supply a human's own lived experience, which is what actually earns reader trust.

## Retail application

This is a direct, practical editing checklist for any retailer using AI to draft product descriptions, buying guides, or blog content — a real risk area as AI content becomes commodity-cheap (see `growth-playbooks`' AI-era Delta #2). The Trust-build step specifically maps to what retail content most often lacks when AI-drafted: genuine product experience, not just spec-sheet paraphrasing — a real staff member's or customer's actual usage notes are exactly the ingredient CRAFT says AI can't supply on its own.

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** A concrete, repeatable editing pass for AI-drafted retail content before publishing; explaining to a team why AI drafts still need substantive human editing, not just a proofread.
**Blind spots:** McCoy's business (Content at Scale) is an AI content tool — her framework is genuinely useful editing discipline, but evaluate any specific tool claims from that source with the awareness that she has a commercial stake in AI content production generally.
