## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Trail-tested hiking and trail-running gear, sold direct and through the independent outdoor shops that actually know the terrain.
**What it does:** Designs and sells technical outerwear, packs, and trail-running gear (jackets, base layers, hydration packs, trail shoes) built and priced for the intermediate hiker/runner who's past their first gear purchase but isn't buying expedition-grade equipment.
**Product category:** Outdoor/adventure apparel and gear.
**Product type:** Physical goods, DTC ecommerce + wholesale.
**Business model:** Product sales — DTC margin on cairnwoodtrail.com, wholesale margin through independent specialty retailers.

## Target Audience
**Target companies:** N/A — B2C brand. (Wholesale buyers are covered under B2B/Wholesale Channel below.)
**Decision-makers:** The end consumer is the sole decision-maker; gift purchases (a meaningful minority around the holidays) are decided by a partner or family member buying for a hiker they know.
**Primary use case:** Outfitting for regular weekend hikes and trail runs — not a single trip, a recurring habit.
**Jobs to be done:**
- Replace gear that's worn out from regular use, not from a single failure
- Get a straight answer on whether a piece will hold up on *their* specific trail conditions, not marketing claims
- Build out a full kit gradually (jacket this season, pack next) without overspending on any one piece
**Use cases:**
- Weekly weekend hikes on a home trail system
- Trail running 2-4x per week, mix of dirt and technical terrain
- Occasional multi-day trips (1-2x per year) that stress-test gear bought for weekly use

## Personas
| Persona | Cares about | Challenge | Value we promise |
|---------|-------------|-----------|------------------|
| The Weekend Regular | Gear that holds up over a full season on the trail they run every week | Can't tell from a product page whether something is actually trail-tested or just photographed well | Gear reviewed and priced for people who use it every weekend, not once a year |
| The Upgrading Beginner | Moving past starter gear bought a year or two ago, now knows what they actually need | Overwhelmed by expedition-grade options priced and built for conditions they'll never hit | A mid-tier line that matches their actual use case, not the top of the catalog |
| The Gift Buyer | Getting a hiker in their life something they'll actually use | Doesn't know the technical differences that matter to a hiker | Clear, non-technical guidance and a return policy that removes the risk of guessing wrong |

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Serious-but-not-extreme hikers and trail runners are stuck choosing between big-box gear that doesn't hold up to regular use and expedition brands priced and engineered for conditions they'll never face.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Big-box outdoor brands: built for occasional use, degrade fast under weekly wear, marketed on price rather than trail performance
- Expedition/technical brands: overbuilt and overpriced for the actual use case, marketing speaks to summit attempts, not Saturday trail runs
**What it costs them:** Repeat purchases of gear that fails within a season, or overpaying for capability they never use.
**Emotional tension:** Wanting to be taken seriously as "a real hiker" without needing gear built for Everest.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Regional outdoor specialty brands at a similar price point — fall short on trail-running-specific product (most are hiking-only, we cover both).
**Secondary:** Big-box sporting goods private labels — fall short on durability and trail-tested credibility; priced lower but the Weekend Regular persona has learned that's a false economy.
**Indirect:** Expedition/technical mountaineering brands — fall short on price and on messaging that doesn't speak to the actual use case; a Weekend Regular feels talked past, not to.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Every product claim is backed by named trail conditions it was tested under, not generic marketing language
- Priced for people who buy gear every season, not once
- Sold through independent specialty retailers who can speak to real trail conditions, not just big-box staff reading a spec sheet
**How we do it differently:** Product copy and reviews are written from actual trail use (see Customer Language below), and the retailer network is deliberately kept to independent specialty shops rather than mass retail, so in-person advice matches the brand's own positioning.
**Why that's better:** Closes the trust gap the Weekend Regular persona names directly — "I want to know it'll actually hold up on the trail I run every Saturday, not just look good in a photo."
**Why customers choose us:** Mid-tier price point with genuine trail-tested credibility neither the big-box nor the expedition-brand alternative can offer at the same time.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Why not just buy from [big-box brand], it's cheaper" | Cost-per-season is lower with Cairnwood even at a higher sticker price — the return/replacement rate on big-box gear under weekly use is the real cost comparison |
| "Isn't this just a smaller/cheaper version of [expedition brand]?" | Not a scaled-down expedition line — built from the ground up for weekly trail use, not summit conditions, so the engineering trade-offs are different, not just less |
| "I can't try it on before buying online" | Free returns within 60 days, and the retailer network exists specifically so a first purchase can happen in person if that matters more |

**Anti-persona:** Expedition/thru-hikers needing certified extreme-condition gear — genuinely not our engineering target, and we say so rather than overclaim.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Gear failure mid-season from a previous big-box purchase; outgrowing starter gear.
**Pull:** A specific, credible trail-conditions claim that matches their own terrain, usually surfaced via a specialty retailer recommendation or a detailed product review.
**Habit:** Once one piece performs well over a full season, the next purchase (building out the rest of the kit) defaults to the same brand without re-shopping.
**Anxiety:** Buying online, sight-unseen, for a purchase that needs to perform reliably outdoors — addressed by the 60-day return policy and retailer network.

## Customer Language
**How they describe the problem:**
- "I want to know it'll actually hold up on the trail I run every Saturday, not just look good in a photo."
- "I don't need summit gear, I need gear that survives my actual Saturday."
**How they describe us:**
- "Finally something built for what I actually do, not what a catalog thinks hiking looks like."
**Words to use:** trail-tested, weekly use, season-tested, straight answer, home trail system.
**Words to avoid:** summit-grade, expedition-rated, extreme, ultimate (all imply the anti-persona's use case, not ours).
**Glossary:**
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Weekend Regular | Primary persona — hikes/runs most weekends, values durability over novelty |
| Trail-tested | Product claim standard: tested under named, real trail conditions before it ships, not lab-only |
| Specialty retailer | An independent outdoor shop stocking Cairnwood — distinct from big-box or marketplace channels |

## Strategic Priorities

**Binding constraint (named per the prioritization method — see `marketing-strategy` skill reference `prioritization.md`):** Conversion rate. Traffic and wholesale interest both currently outpace DTC checkout conversion — the leak is at the point of purchase, not the top of the funnel.

**Candidates considered and scored** (constraint fit / confidence / reach, each 1-5):

| Candidate priority | Constraint fit | Confidence | Reach | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trail-conditions proof on every PDP (photos, named conditions, durability data) | 5 | 4 | 5 | **Kept** — directly targets the trust gap named in Customer Language |
| Rebuild checkout flow, cut steps | 5 | 4 | 4 | **Kept** — directly addresses the binding constraint |
| Expand specialty retailer count 120 → 200 | 2 | 3 | 3 | Cut |
| Launch a loyalty/points program | 2 | 2 | 3 | Cut |
| Paid social prospecting expansion | 2 | 3 | 4 | Cut |
| Trail-running product line expansion | 3 | 3 | 4 | Cut this cycle, revisit next |

**This cycle's priorities:**
1. Rebuild the DTC checkout flow to remove friction — directly targets the binding constraint (conversion rate), highest confidence, touches all DTC revenue.
2. Add named trail-conditions proof (photos, test conditions, durability data) to every core product page — addresses the trust gap that is the actual reason browsers don't convert, not a generic CRO tactic.

**Explicitly deprioritized (and why):**
- Expanding the specialty retailer network — doesn't address the binding constraint; wholesale isn't where the conversion problem lives this cycle.
- Loyalty/points program — low confidence it moves conversion specifically; a retention play for a different constraint.
- Paid social prospecting expansion — adds more traffic into a checkout flow that's already leaking; fixing the leak comes first.
- Trail-running product line expansion — real opportunity, but a product-development timeline, not a marketing-cycle priority; revisit next cycle once conversion is fixed.

**Binding constraint:** Conversion rate (see above).

## Goals
**Business goal:** Grow DTC revenue 20% this cycle without increasing paid acquisition spend, by fixing checkout conversion rather than buying more traffic into it.
**Conversion action:** Completed DTC checkout.
**Current metrics:** DTC checkout conversion rate currently trails the category benchmark; cart abandonment concentrated at the shipping-cost reveal step.

## Brand Tier & Price Positioning
**Tier:** Mid-tier / premium-accessible — priced above big-box private label, below expedition/technical mountaineering brands.
**Price position vs. direct competitors:** At parity with regional outdoor specialty competitors; the differentiation is trail-tested credibility and retailer relationships, not price.
**Distribution/channel signal:** Independent specialty retailers only (no big-box, no discount marketplace) — the channel choice itself signals the mid-tier positioning, and any expansion into mass retail would contradict it.

## Distribution Model
**Model:** Hybrid — DTC ecommerce (own site, ~65% of revenue) + wholesale through independent outdoor specialty retailers (~30%) + a small regional multi-brand outdoor co-op channel (~5%).
**MAP/dealer constraints:** Minimum advertised price enforced across all wholesale accounts to protect retailer margin and the mid-tier price signal — DTC price never undercuts wholesale MAP.
**Channel overlap:** DTC and wholesale sell the same core line; wholesale-exclusive colorways exist specifically so specialty retailers have something DTC doesn't, protecting the reason they stock the brand.

## B2B / Wholesale Channel
**Applies?:** Yes.
**What's sold / to whom:** Full core product line sold at wholesale margin to independent outdoor specialty retailers — not big-box, not marketplace.
**How central to the business:** Meaningful but secondary — 30% of revenue, and central to brand credibility (specialty retailer endorsement is a proof point DTC marketing leans on) even where it isn't central to revenue.
