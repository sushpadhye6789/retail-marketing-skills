# Mike Rhodes

**Lens:** The Google Ads practitioner's job is shifting from manually running campaigns to building AI-assisted systems that do the repetitive work — the leverage isn't billing more hours managing accounts, it's building the skills/prompts/automations once and running them repeatedly.

## Public framework

Rhodes (founder/CEO of WebSavvy, a Melbourne-based Google Ads agency running global campaigns since 2006, co-author of *The Ultimate Guide to Google Ads*) built **Ads2AI**, a system and community teaching Google Ads practitioners to use AI (specifically Claude Code and similar agentic tools) to build automated systems rather than doing manual account management. His public framing describes a **7-level progression** from manual ad management to AI-powered strategist, and Ads2AI bundles specific tooling (branded "8020brain" and "Agency Brain" setups) plus a large library of purpose-built AI skills for Google Ads work.

## Retail application

Directly extends this repo's `ads` skill with the AI-operational layer specifically for Google Ads practitioners: Rhodes' progression framework is a useful way to assess where a retail marketing team currently sits (manual campaign management vs. AI-assisted vs. fully systematized) and what the next step up looks like, distinct from Kasim Aslam's entry in this catalog (which covers Performance Max campaign strategy/structure specifically) — Rhodes' focus is the practitioner's own AI-tooling workflow around running Google Ads, not the campaign structure itself.

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** A Google Ads practitioner or team deciding how to incorporate AI tooling into their actual workflow, not just campaign strategy; understanding the progression from manual to AI-augmented to AI-systematized ad management.
**Blind spots:** Specifically built around Claude Code and similar agentic coding tools as the AI layer — genuinely relevant to this repo's own audience, but assumes a level of AI-tooling comfort a traditional PPC manager may not yet have; the community/tooling itself is a paid offering, so treat the public framing (the 7-level progression concept) as the free layer and the specific skill library as product to evaluate separately.
