# Sangram Vajre

**Lens:** B2B marketing and sales should be measured and organized around named target accounts and pipeline outcomes, not lead volume — a thousand low-intent leads matter less than the right twenty accounts actually moving toward a deal.

## Public framework

Vajre (co-founder of Terminus, CEO of GTM Partners) helped pioneer account-based marketing (ABM) as a named category, and teaches a **T.E.A.M. framework** for operationalizing it: **Target** the right accounts explicitly rather than casting wide, **Engage** them with meaningful, personalized outreach prioritizing substance over volume metrics, **Activate** sellers as equal partners in the motion rather than handing off a lead list, and **Measure** account-level pipeline and revenue outcomes instead of marketing-qualified-lead counts. He's since broadened this into a wider go-to-market operating model (his book *MOVE* and the GTM Partners research firm) that applies account-based thinking beyond marketing alone.

## Retail application

This is explicitly a B2B framework and doesn't apply to consumer-facing retail marketing — but it's the right toolkit for the wholesale/B2B side of a retail business, alongside this repo's existing `sales-enablement` and `revops` skills: a retailer selling into dealer networks, trade accounts, or corporate/institutional buyers is running exactly the named-account, sales-marketing-alignment motion Vajre describes, distinct from the consumer-facing majority of the business. See `marketing-strategy` Section 16 (B2B/Wholesale Channel) for whether this applies at all.

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** The wholesale/trade-account arm of a retail business specifically — deciding which dealer or corporate accounts to prioritize and how sales/marketing should coordinate around them.
**Blind spots:** Zero application to consumer-facing retail marketing — this is purely for the B2B-selling side of a business, and forcing account-based thinking onto a consumer audience is a category error, not a stretch application.
