# Worked example: a taste record for a fictional retailer

Harbour & Hearth is a fictional Australian homewares retailer with an online store, one showroom and a trade arm. This is the shape of a finished record, not a set of rules to copy. A real record comes from the leader's own answers, and every line carries a tag: `(seen)` they watched it happen in their own results, `(belief)` they hold it from experience, `(lore)` it is industry convention.

## Copy that works
- Open with the product and what it is used for, not with the discount. (seen)
- Short sentences, one idea each, written as if to a neighbour. (belief)
- Distrust headlines that promise "transform your home". Prefer a plain statement of the thing. (belief)

## Messaging that works
- Customers believe "made to last" when we show the warranty and the repair service. (seen)
- Proof that lands: a named maker, a real material, a real dimension. (belief)
- Words to own: "everyday", "repairable". Words to avoid: "luxury", "curated". (belief)

## Angles
- Quality and longevity beat price for the core customer. Price leads only for new-to-brand customers. (seen)
- Gifting works in the three weeks before Christmas and Mother's Day, and nowhere else. (seen)

## Channels
- Email is for returning customers and range news. Never ask it to win new customers. (belief)
- The showroom window sells the story. The product page sells the specification. (lore)

## Tactics
- Bundles of three work for tableware and fail for furniture. (seen)
- Hero lines stay at full price until the final markdown stage. Clearance starts with broken ranges. (belief)
- A loyalty-only preview before a sale lifts full-price sales of the new range. (seen)

## Nuances
- Autumn in Melbourne means cooking and layering, not entertaining. (belief)
- Trade customers order in quarter-end bursts. Do not email them mid-month. (seen)

## Never
- Never put a clearance badge on a hero product. (belief)
- Never use stock photos of people in the range. (belief)
- Never write "limited stock" unless the stock is genuinely limited. (lore)

## Examples
- Right: "Hand-glazed stoneware bowls, made in Bendigo. Dishwasher safe, five-year chip warranty." Wrong: "Elevate your dining experience with our stunning new collection." The right one names the thing, the maker and the proof.
- Right: a window with six pieces and a price on each. Wrong: a window of forty pieces and a sale banner. The right one shows a point of view and what to buy.

**Notes on this example.** The last Never line is also a compliance matter. A taste record can say it, but `compliance` owns the rule about genuine scarcity claims. The record never contains a margin floor, a required disclaimer or an approval rule, because those do not belong to taste and no taste rule can waive them.
