# Kartik Hosanagar

**Lens:** Algorithmic personalization systems shape consumer behavior and choice in ways users often don't recognize — using them responsibly requires understanding not just how to build effective personalization, but how it actually changes what people buy and believe.

## Public framework

Hosanagar (Wharton Professor of Technology and Digital Business, author of *A Human's Guide to Machine Intelligence*) researches the digital economy's algorithmic systems — personalization, recommendation engines, and algorithmic decision-making — with particular focus on how AI-driven personalized product recommendations and dynamic pricing affect real consumer behavior, not just conversion metrics in isolation. His academic research (published work co-authored with Dokyun Lee on AI in personalized product recommendations) and his book examine both the mechanics of algorithmic personalization and its second-order effects — filter bubbles, unintended bias, and the ethical questions of using AI to influence purchase decisions at scale.

## Retail application

This adds a research-grounded caution layer to whatever a retailer's personalization or dynamic-pricing system is doing: Hosanagar's work is a useful check on whether a recommendation engine or dynamic pricing model is actually serving the customer's interest, or optimizing for short-term conversion in a way that erodes trust once noticed (e.g., personalization that narrows product discovery, or dynamic pricing perceived as unfair). Relevant to `product-feed`'s personalization angle and any AI-driven recommendation work a retailer builds.

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Thinking through the second-order and ethical effects of a personalization or dynamic-pricing system before shipping it, not just its immediate conversion lift; understanding algorithmic-bias risk in recommendation systems.
**Blind spots:** Academic and research-oriented rather than a tactical implementation guide — useful for the "should we, and how carefully" questions around personalization, not the specific engineering of a recommendation system.

## Go deeper

*A Human's Guide to Machine Intelligence* and his Wharton course "AI Applications in Marketing and Finance" carry the fuller research and case analysis beyond this summary.
