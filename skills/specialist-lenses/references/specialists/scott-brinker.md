# Scott Brinker

**Lens:** The martech landscape's sheer scale (over 15,000 tracked products) means the real strategic skill isn't picking the perfect stack once — it's designing a platform ecosystem that balances centralized data/control with the decentralized, fast-moving experimentation individual marketing teams need.

## Public framework

Brinker (VP of Platform Ecosystems at HubSpot, editor of chiefmartec.com) publishes the widely-cited annual **Marketing Technology Landscape** ("martech supergraphic") tracking the category at large scale, alongside recurring **State of Martech** research. His core strategic argument, consistent across his writing: martech strategy is fundamentally about platform-ecosystem design — deciding what stays centralized (data governance, core customer records, compliance) versus what stays decentralized (individual teams' tool choices for their own experimentation) — rather than a single "best stack" decision made once and left static. He's also tracked the martech market's own maturation, noting the landscape's growth recently plateauing after 15 years of continuous expansion, with significant underlying churn (new tools added, old ones retired) even as the total count stabilizes.

## Retail application

Directly useful framing for any retailer's own martech decisions: rather than treating "which CDP/ESP/personalization tool" as one big platform choice, Brinker's centralize-vs-decentralize lens helps decide which systems need to be the single source of truth (customer data, order history) versus which can be swapped or run in parallel by individual teams without breaking anything else. Useful context for `analytics` and any tool-selection decision in this repo's `tools/` registry.

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Deciding what belongs in a centralized, governed data layer versus what individual marketing functions can choose independently; understanding the current martech market's maturity level when evaluating new tools.
**Blind spots:** A strategic/architectural lens on tool ecosystems, not a tool-selection guide for any specific category — pairs with `tools/REGISTRY.md`'s actual tool comparisons rather than replacing them.
