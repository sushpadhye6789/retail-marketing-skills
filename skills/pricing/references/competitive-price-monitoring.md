# Competitive & Dealer Price Monitoring

Ongoing price checking is a different job from the one-time research methods elsewhere in this skill (Van Westendorp, MaxDiff answer "what should we charge"; this answers "what is everyone actually charging right now, and is our own channel priced consistently"). Two distinct checks:

## 1. Competitor Price Checking

**What to check**: current price, current promotion/discount depth, and any bundle/packaging differences for the same or comparable SKUs across the competitor set identified in `marketing-strategy` Section 5 (Competitive Landscape). A price comparison that ignores packaging differences (a competitor's "lower" price on a smaller pack size, or missing a component your bundle includes) produces a misleading read — normalize to a comparable unit before concluding you're over- or under-priced.

**Cadence**: match check frequency to how often the category actually moves — a category with frequent promotional cycling (many consumer categories) needs more frequent checks than a stable B2B/considered-purchase category. A one-time check answers "where do we stand today"; a recurring check (weekly/monthly, or event-triggered around known promotional calendar dates) is what actually informs pricing decisions, since a single snapshot can catch a competitor mid-promotion and misread it as their everyday price.

**What to do with findings**:
- Feed into `price-elasticity` before reacting — a competitor being cheaper doesn't automatically mean you should cut price; whether it matters depends on your own elasticity and margin, not just the gap.
- Feed into `marketing-strategy` Section 5/6 if the competitive landscape or differentiation story has shifted materially, not just the price.
- Feed into `offers`/`discount-and-clearance` if the finding is specifically about promotional cadence or depth, not everyday pricing.

**For deeper competitor context beyond price** (positioning, feature set, messaging), see `competitor-profiling` and `competitors` — this section is a narrower, recurring slice of that broader competitive intelligence work, focused specifically and repeatedly on price.

## 2. Dealer / Channel Price Consistency (MAP Compliance)

Relevant only if `marketing-strategy` Section 15 (Distribution Model) names a dealer network — see `marketing-strategy/references/distribution-model-guide.md` for the fuller channel-conflict picture this feeds into.

**What to check**: are dealers actually advertising at or above the agreed MAP (minimum advertised price)? Note the distinction between MAP (the advertised/listed price) and actual transaction price, which most MAP agreements don't control — a dealer can legally sell below MAP in-store or via private negotiation while still complying, as long as the *advertised* price meets the floor. Don't conflate the two when assessing compliance.

**Cadence**: spot-checks around known high-promotion periods (major sales events) catch the highest-risk windows; a lighter ongoing check catches drift outside those windows.

**What to do with findings**: a MAP violation is a channel-relationship issue as much as a pricing one — how it's raised with the dealer matters as much as catching it. This skill's job is surfacing the finding; resolving it is a relationship/contract conversation outside this skill's scope. If violations are frequent enough to suggest the MAP level itself is unrealistic (dealers routinely can't sell at it and stay competitive locally), that's worth flagging as a pricing-strategy question, not just a compliance one.

---

*No specific competitor names, price points, or "typical" MAP violation rates are asserted here — every number in a real check has to come from the actual current market, not a general pattern stated in this reference.*
