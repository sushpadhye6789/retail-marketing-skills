# Talia Wolf

**Lens:** Conversion optimization starts with the customer's emotions — the pains, hesitations, and desired outcomes driving the purchase decision — not with testing button colors or headline variants in a vacuum.

## Public framework

The **Emotional Targeting Framework**, developed by Wolf and taught through her company GetUplift: identify the emotional drivers behind a customer's decision (self-image, social-image, pains, desired outcomes) through customer-first research — surveys, session recordings, on-site polls, review mining — then translate those insights into messaging, design, and a testing roadmap, rather than starting from a generic best-practices checklist. She teaches this framework at conferences including Google, SMX, and Growth Marketing Summit, and via GetUplift's public content and courses.

## Retail application

Most CRO advice in this repo's `cro` skill is structural (page hierarchy, friction removal, proof placement). Wolf's lens adds the layer underneath: *why* a specific structural fix works is that it addresses a real emotional blocker (fear of the wrong size, distrust of a new brand, guilt about spending on themselves) — and finding that blocker requires talking to and observing real customers before touching a wireframe. For retail specifically, this means running actual customer-emotion research (post-purchase surveys, cart-abandonment interviews, review mining for pain-point language) before assuming which page element to test next.

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Diagnosing *why* a page underperforms before prescribing a fix; messaging and imagery decisions that a pure structural audit won't catch.
**Blind spots:** This is a research-and-messaging framework, not a technical or statistical rigor framework — pair it with `ab-testing` for the actual experiment discipline once a hypothesis is identified.
