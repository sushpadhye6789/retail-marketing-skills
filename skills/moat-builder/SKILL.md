# Moat Builder

You help users identify which of their competitive advantages are actually durable — structural moats that get stronger over time and are hard for a competitor to replicate quickly — versus which are temporary advantages (a good ad, a clever launch, a price promotion) that any competitor can copy within a quarter. Then you direct marketing investment specifically toward widening the real ones.

**Scope note**: `marketing-strategy` Section 6 (Differentiation) captures what's different about the business for positioning purposes — this skill asks a harder question of that same material: which of these differentiators is actually a moat, and what does marketing need to *do*, on an ongoing basis, to keep it widening rather than eroding.

## Before Starting

**Check for product marketing context first:**
If `.agents/marketing-strategy.md` exists, read it — Sections 5 (Competitive Landscape) and 6 (Differentiation) are the direct input. Check whether Section 12 (Strategic Priorities) already reflects moat-building as a priority, or whether it should.

Gather this context (ask if not provided):

### 1. Candidate Advantages
- What does the business currently point to as a differentiator or advantage?
- How long has each one held, and has a competitor come close to replicating any of them?

### 2. Investment Appetite
- Is there appetite to bias strategic priorities and budget toward a moat-building initiative that may not show near-term ROAS, in exchange for a compounding long-term advantage?

## Moat Types and How Marketing Widens Each

A real moat gets stronger (or at least holds) over time and compounds — as distinct from an advantage that's simply true today and could evaporate the moment a competitor decides to match it.

### Network effects
The product or brand gets more valuable as more people use it (marketplaces, community-driven products, some loyalty ecosystems).
**Widen it via**: `referrals` (directly grows the network), `community-marketing` (deepens engagement among existing members, which is itself part of the network's value), `loyalty` if the program has a social/status component that gets more valuable with more members.

### Switching costs
Customers face real friction leaving — data lock-in, learned habits, integrated workflows, accumulated loyalty status or history.
**Widen it via**: `onboarding` depth (the more invested a customer becomes early, the higher the switching cost), `loyalty` program design (status and history that would be lost by switching), `churn-prevention`'s retention discipline more broadly.

### Brand / intangible assets
Trust, prestige, or perceived quality that commands a price premium or preference independent of feature comparison.
**Widen it via**: `brand-management` (health tracking and consistent governance over time), `tentpole-campaign` (sustained brand investment), `storytelling` (the narrative that builds the intangible asset in the first place), consistent `brand-guidelines` execution — a brand moat erodes fast under inconsistent execution and is compounded by disciplined consistency over years, not a single campaign. Directly connected to `marketing-strategy` Section 14 — premium/luxury tier positioning is, in large part, an attempt to build and protect exactly this kind of moat.

### Proprietary data / owned audience
First-party customer data, an owned email/SMS list, or purchase-history depth that improves personalization or product over time in a way competitors without that data can't replicate.
**Widen it via**: `attribution`'s first-party tracking pillar, deliberate email/SMS list-building (`emails`/`sms`), `loyalty` program data capture, `customer-research` depth compounding over time into a real understanding-of-customer advantage.

### Cost / scale advantages
A lower cost structure from volume, supply relationships, or operational efficiency that supports sustainably lower prices or higher margin than competitors at the same price point.
**Widen it via**: marketing's role here is more indirect (this is mostly an operations/supply-chain lever) — but `price-elasticity`-informed pricing that actually exploits the cost advantage rather than leaving margin on the table, and an everyday-low-price positioning if that's the deliberate strategy (see `marketing-strategy/references/brand-tier-guide.md`'s Budget row), are the marketing-side levers.

### Exclusive distribution / supply relationships
Locked-in exclusive dealer or supplier relationships, or exclusive product access competitors can't get.
**Widen it via**: `supplier-funded-marketing` relationship investment, `distribution-model-guide.md`-aware coordination, `range-review` decisions that deliberately protect exclusivity rather than diluting it.

### Regulatory / IP protection
Patents, certifications, or regulatory approval barriers that block or slow competitor entry.
**Widen it via**: marketing's role is communicating the protected claim credibly — see `compliance` directly, since a protected/exclusive claim needs real substantiation behind it, more so than an ordinary marketing claim, precisely because it's being used to assert a durable advantage.

## Assessing Moat Strength

For each candidate, ask:

- **How long would it take a well-funded, motivated competitor to replicate this?** Weeks (not a moat), a year or more (a real moat), or effectively never (a very strong moat).
- **Is it getting stronger or eroding right now?** A moat isn't a one-time achievement — network effects and data moats compound if actively reinforced and can stagnate or erode if neglected; a brand moat decays under inconsistent execution.
- **Does it compound, or is it static?** A good ad campaign is static — it doesn't get more valuable over time. A network effect or a growing first-party data asset compounds. This distinction is the core test for whether something is actually a moat versus just a current advantage.

---

## Directing Investment to the Moat

The point of this exercise is to change what gets prioritized, not just to produce an interesting assessment. Once a real moat (or moat candidate worth building) is identified:

- It should show up explicitly in `marketing-strategy` Section 12 (Strategic Priorities) — use `prioritization.md`'s method there to weigh moat-building against other candidate priorities honestly, including against the binding constraint.
- `budget-allocation` and `channel-selection` should be biased toward the specific activities that widen the identified moat, not spread evenly across every growth lever that shows good near-term ROAS — a moat-building initiative often won't show the same short-term return as a performance campaign, and that's an expected tradeoff of building something durable, not a sign it's failing.

## Common Mistakes

- **Confusing a temporary advantage for a durable moat** — a good ad, a viral moment, or a one-time price promotion doesn't compound and can be matched fast; see Assessing Moat Strength's compounding test.
- **Trying to build multiple moat types at once with no focus** — the same failure mode `prioritization.md` warns about for strategic priorities generally: spreading investment across everything means nothing gets wide enough to matter.
- **Claiming a moat that doesn't structurally exist** — a "quality" or "better" claim with no structural backing (no real switching cost, no real data advantage, no real exclusivity) isn't a moat, it's a positioning claim; don't let this skill's framing be used to over-assert durability that isn't real.
- **Ignoring moat erosion** — treating a moat as a permanent, one-time achievement rather than something that needs active reinforcement (a brand moat under inconsistent execution, a network effect with stalled growth) to actually hold.

## Task-Specific Questions

1. What does the business currently point to as its main advantage(s), and how long has each held?
2. Has a competitor ever come close to replicating any of them, and how fast?
3. Which moat type(s) does this actually map to, if any?
4. Is there appetite to prioritize moat-building over near-term ROAS-optimized activity?
5. Does `marketing-strategy` Section 12 already reflect this, or does it need to be added?
