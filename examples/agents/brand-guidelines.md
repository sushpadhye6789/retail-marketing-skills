# Brand Guidelines

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-09-04

> **Worked example, not a real business.** Same fictional brand as `examples/agents/marketing-strategy.md` — Cairnwood Trail Co. Copy this to `.agents/brand-guidelines.md` to use it, or better, run the `brand-guidelines` skill to build your own. `.agents/` is gitignored on purpose.

## Logo
**Primary:** Wordmark "Cairnwood" set in the headline typeface, with a small stacked-stone (cairn) mark as the icon variant.
**Variants:** Full wordmark + icon (default), icon-only (favicons, app icons, small social avatars).
**Clear space:** Minimum clear space equal to the height of the icon mark on all sides.
**Minimum size:** Icon-only variant not used below 24px — below that, the stacked-stone mark stops reading clearly.
**Backgrounds:** Full-color mark on light/neutral backgrounds; single-color (ink) reversed-white mark on photo backgrounds or dark trail-green.
**Don'ts:**
- Don't recolor the icon mark outside the approved palette
- Don't place the full wordmark on a busy trail-photo background without a solid-color safe area behind it
- Don't stretch or rotate either variant

## Color Palette
**Primary:** Trail Green — #2B4436
**Secondary:** Warm Stone — #C9BBA0
**Accent:** Rust Ochre — #B5622B (CTAs and sale/urgent flags only, not a dominant color)
**Usage hierarchy:** Trail Green dominates (headers, primary buttons); Warm Stone for backgrounds/cards; Rust Ochre sparingly, for the one thing on a page that should pull the eye.
**Tier-alignment note:** muted, earth-toned palette with restrained saturation matches Cairnwood's mid-tier positioning (per `marketing-strategy` Section 14) — deliberately not the bright, high-contrast palette a budget/mass-market outdoor brand would use.

## Typography
**Headline typeface:** A humanist sans-serif with slightly rounded terminals (e.g., a font in the Söhne/Inter family) — reads technical without feeling cold.
**Body typeface:** Same family, regular weight, for body copy and specs.
**Fallbacks:** System sans-serif stack for email/platforms that don't support the primary face.
**Hierarchy:**
| Element | Typeface | Size/Weight |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Headline face | Bold, large |
| H2/H3 | Headline face | Semibold, medium |
| Body | Body face | Regular |
| Caption/label | Body face | Regular, smaller, slight letter-spacing |

## Imagery Style
**Photography style:** Lifestyle-first — real trail conditions, real weather, the Weekend Regular persona's actual use case. Product-on-white only for spec/comparison contexts (size charts, PDP detail shots), never as the primary hero image.
**Color treatment:** Natural color, not heavily filtered — consistent with "trail-tested, not staged" positioning.
**Illustration style:** Minimal — used only for technical diagrams (trail-conditions icons, care instructions), never for hero or lifestyle content.
**Avoid:** Studio-perfect "catalog" shots with no visible trail context; generic stock-photo hikers who don't match the Weekend Regular or Upgrading Beginner personas.

## Voice & Tone
**Tone:** Straight-talking, specific, earns trust through detail rather than superlatives.
**Communication style:** Short, concrete claims tied to named trail conditions. No summit-conquest language.
**Personality:** The experienced friend who hikes the same trail as you every weekend, not a mountaineering guide.
**Words to use:** trail-tested, weekly use, season-tested, straight answer, home trail system.
**Words to avoid:** summit-grade, expedition-rated, extreme, ultimate (all imply the anti-persona's use case, not Cairnwood's — see `marketing-strategy` Section 7, Anti-persona).
**Channel flex:** Slightly warmer/more conversational on social and email (still specific, never generic-playful); most restrained and spec-forward on PDPs, where a buying decision is being made.
**Tier-alignment check:** no exclamation-heavy urgency language or constant "limited time" framing — that would contradict the mid-tier, trail-tested-not-hyped positioning the whole brand rests on.

## Do's and Don'ts
- Do name specific trail conditions in every durability claim ("held up through a full wet-weather season on the Weekend Regular's home trail"), never a generic durability adjective alone
- Do use the Rust Ochre accent for exactly one element per layout — more than that and nothing pulls focus
- Don't use big-box or expedition-brand comparison language ("better than the leading brand") — the differentiation is trail-tested credibility, not a head-to-head claim
- Don't crop the icon mark or use it smaller than 24px
