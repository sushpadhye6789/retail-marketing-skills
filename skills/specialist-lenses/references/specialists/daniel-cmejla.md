# Daniel Cmejla

**Lens:** A community becomes a genuine competitive moat when relationships live inside it — the switching cost isn't the product anymore, it's the peer network the customer would lose by leaving, which a feature-based competitor can't replicate by copying functionality.

## Public framework

Cmejla (VP of Community at Apollo, previously built the Modern Sales Pros community from 1,000 to 14,000 members at Chili Piper) is a practitioner of community-led growth specifically as a growth-engine strategy, not a support-cost-reduction one. At Apollo, community-led activity is publicly credited with over 75% of customer acquisition. His public talks and interviews describe the compounding mechanic explicitly: a community gets more valuable to each member as more people join, and the relationships formed inside it create switching costs a competitor's feature parity can't undo. **Confidence note:** the "Human Return" framing referenced in the source directory wasn't independently confirmed in this research pass as his specific coined term — the underlying community-as-moat argument is well documented, but verify that exact label against his current public output before quoting it as his own terminology.

## Retail application

This directly extends this repo's `community-marketing` skill with a moat-specific argument: a retail brand's community (a fan forum, a members-only group, an ambassador network — see `growth-playbooks`' Community-Led & UGC Flywheels entry for brand examples like Glossier and Gymshark) creates real defensibility when it fosters genuine peer relationships, not just a channel for the brand to broadcast into. The 75%+-of-acquisition figure, while specific to Apollo's B2B context, is a useful benchmark for how significant a well-run community channel can become relative to paid acquisition.

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Making the case for community investment as a defensibility strategy, not just an engagement nice-to-have; understanding what makes a community sticky (peer relationships) versus what doesn't (brand content alone).
**Blind spots:** His direct experience is B2B SaaS community-building (Chili Piper, Apollo) — the switching-cost-via-relationships mechanic generalizes to retail, but the specific tactics (professional networking community formats) need retail-appropriate translation.
