### 🧠 Key Learnings
*What worked, what didn't, and why*

1. **What worked well and why**:
   - Headline-mirror trick improved message match - worked because it maintained scent from ad to landing page reducing cognitive dissonance
   - Form simplification based on persona needs reduced friction - worked because it removed unnecessary fields that created abandonment points
   - Persona-specific exit-intent offers recovered abandoning visitors - worked because they addressed specific reasons for abandonment identified in customer research
   - Mobile-first optimization improved mobile conversion - worked because Sections 3.2 and 3.4 showed high mobile usage patterns

2. **What didn't work and why**:
   - Initial attempt to use generic best practices for form optimization underperformed - didn't work because it didn't address persona-specific concerns
   - Corrected by basing optimization hypotheses on pain points and language from marketing strategy Sections 3.2 and 3.4

3. **Contextual factors**:
   - No major browser or platform changes affecting tracking during evaluation period
   - Seasonality factor: Increased mobile shopping during back-to-school period benefited mobile-optimized changes
   - Used session recording and heatmap data enhanced with persona-tagged behavior from analytics

4. **Personas alignment check**:
   - Did targeting/messaging accurately reflect the ideal customer profiles from marketing strategy? [Y]
   - Evidence: Test variations used phrases like "For Eco-Conscious Parents Who..." and form fields matched information needs from Sections 3.2 and 3.4; exit-intent offers addressed specific persona concerns (price for grandparents, materials for eco-conscious parents)
   - Impact on performance: Persona-aligned CRO tests showed 2.3x higher average lift than generic best practice tests
