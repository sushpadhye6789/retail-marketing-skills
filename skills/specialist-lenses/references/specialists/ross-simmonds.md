# Ross Simmonds

**Lens:** Most content marketing over-invests in creation and under-invests in distribution — flip the ratio, and a smaller pool of high-value content, repurposed and redistributed relentlessly, outperforms a larger pool published once and left alone.

## Public framework

Simmonds (founder of Foundation Marketing) lays out **"Create Once, Distribute Forever"** in his book of the same name: instead of a constant treadmill of new content, invest in genuinely high-utility pieces, then remix and redistribute them repeatedly across channels — different formats, different platforms, different angles on the same underlying research or idea — for long-term return rather than one-time traffic. He pairs this with an explicit maintenance discipline: revisit and update older high-value content (refreshing stats, examples) rather than letting it decay, since the redistribution strategy only works on content that's still accurate.

## Retail application

This directly extends `content-strategy`'s existing scope: a single well-researched buying guide or product-education piece can be redistributed as social posts, an email sequence, a video script, and updated seasonally — rather than treating each channel as needing its own from-scratch content. For a retailer with limited content-production capacity, this reframes the question from "what should we create next" to "what have we already created that we haven't fully distributed yet."

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Getting more return from an already-thin content budget by redistributing existing high-value pieces before creating new ones; auditing whether a content library's failure to perform is a creation problem or a distribution problem.
**Blind spots:** The framework was built primarily for B2B/SaaS content marketing at Foundation Marketing's client base — the *distribution* principle generalizes well to retail, but the specific channel mix (dark social, Reddit, Discord) skews toward B2B software audiences and needs retail-appropriate substitution (relevant retail communities, platform-native social formats) rather than direct copying.
