# Community Models — Which Structure Fits a Retail Business

The Platform Selection Guide in the main skill file answers *where* to build (Discord, Slack, Circle, Reddit, etc.). This doc answers a question that comes first: *what kind of community is this, structurally* — because the platform choice, the content that sustains it, and the metrics that mean anything all follow from that answer. Picking a platform before naming the model is how a brand ends up with a Discord server that looks like every SaaS product's Discord server, regardless of whether that's the right shape for a retail audience.

## The six models

### 1. VIP / Loyalty community

Membership is tied to a loyalty program tier or a spend/purchase threshold, not open enrollment. The community *is* the top tier's benefit, not a separate initiative.

- **Best fit:** Brands with a loyalty program already live (see `loyalty`) and repeat-purchase categories (beauty, apparel, food/beverage) where a top-tier customer has real lifetime value to protect.
- **What it needs:** A gating mechanism tied to the loyalty program, genuinely exclusive content or early access (not just a badge), and a reason to check in that isn't purely transactional.
- **Watch out for:** Building the gate before there's anything exclusive behind it — an "elite" tier with nothing different from the regular list feels like a bait-and-switch.
- **Cross-reference:** `loyalty` owns the program mechanics and tiering; this model is the community layer on top of it.

### 2. Ambassador / Advocate network

A curated, invite-only group of existing customers who already recommend the brand unprompted. Smaller and higher-touch than an open community — dozens to low hundreds, not thousands.

- **Best fit:** Any retail category, but especially ones where genuine enthusiasm is visible before the program exists (reviews, unprompted social mentions, community posts elsewhere).
- **What it needs:** Personal recruitment (see the main skill file's Ambassador Program playbook), meaningful non-cash benefits, and content/tools that make sharing easy.
- **Watch out for:** Treating it as a discount-code distribution list — an ambassador network that only exists to hand out codes isn't a community, it's an affiliate program with extra steps (see `referrals` for the affiliate/referral mechanics that model actually needs).

### 3. UGC / Content community

Members join to share and see how other customers use, wear, style, or prepare the product. The community's value is peer-generated content, not brand-generated content.

- **Best fit:** Visually or experientially expressive categories — apparel, beauty, home decor, food/beverage (recipes), hobbyist/craft goods. Weak fit for categories with little visible variation in how the product is used (a commodity household staple, for instance).
- **What it needs:** Low-friction sharing (a hashtag, a tagged-photo feature, a Discord/Circle gallery channel), regular spotlighting of member content, and a reason to browse even when not posting.
- **Watch out for:** A community that only works one direction (brand asks, members post, brand takes the content for ads without credit or reciprocity) burns out fast — see the main skill file's "value must flow to members first" principle.

### 4. Peer-support community

Members help each other with product use, troubleshooting, or category knowledge — the brand's support team participates but doesn't carry the whole load.

- **Best fit:** Categories with a real learning curve or ongoing use complexity: durable goods, technical/outdoor gear, appliances, anything with setup/maintenance/technique. Weak fit for simple, low-consideration purchases with nothing to trouble-shoot.
- **What it needs:** A searchable structure (Discourse/forum formats hold up better here than fast-scrolling chat), staff presence to fill knowledge gaps without dominating the conversation, and recognition for members who consistently help others (see the main skill file's Community-Led Support playbook).
- **Watch out for:** Letting unanswered questions pile up — a peer-support community that doesn't reliably get questions answered stops being worth checking.

### 5. Category / Lifestyle community

Built around the identity and interest the product serves, not the product itself — the community would still make sense to its members even if the brand's name were removed from the header. The main skill file's "build around a shared identity" principle taken to its fullest form.

- **Best fit:** Brands with a strong lifestyle or activity attached to the category (running, home cooking, home fitness, outdoor/adventure gear) where the interest is bigger than any one brand's product line.
- **What it needs:** Real investment in content and events that serve the identity, not just the product (see `experiential-marketing`'s Community Workshops section for the in-person counterpart) — a running-shoe brand's community should behave like a running club that happens to sell shoes, not a shoe-launch-announcement channel.
- **Watch out for:** The highest-effort, highest-payoff model on this list, and also the easiest to under-invest in and have fail quietly — don't start here unless there's real budget and content commitment behind it.

### 6. Trade / Wholesale account community

A private community for retail buyers, dealers, or wholesale accounts rather than end consumers — order updates, new-line previews, peer discussion among accounts who don't compete directly (different territories/channels).

- **Best fit:** Brands with an active B2B/wholesale channel (see `marketing-strategy` Section 16) and enough accounts to make a shared space worthwhile — usually the Commercial segment rather than a handful of Business-segment accounts who are better served one-to-one.
- **What it needs:** A platform buyers will actually check (Slack fits better than Discord for a professional-buyer audience — see the main skill file's Platform Selection Guide), genuinely useful content (sell-through data, early access to new lines, co-op marketing assets), and a clear boundary from the consumer-facing community.
- **Watch out for:** Merging this with the consumer community "to keep things simple" — a wholesale buyer and a retail shopper want different content and different tone; see `marketing-strategy` Section 16's guidance on keeping B2C and B2B from interfering with each other.

## Choosing a model

Most retail brands run more than one model at once, at different scales — a large ambassador network feeding a smaller VIP tier, or a category/lifestyle community with a peer-support sub-channel. Don't force a single model; name which one is primary and which are secondary, and size the effort accordingly. The main skill file's intake question 4 (primary community goal) is the fastest way to narrow the list: retention/repeat-purchase points toward VIP/Loyalty, word-of-mouth points toward Ambassador, support deflection points toward Peer-support, and a broader brand-building goal points toward Category/Lifestyle.
