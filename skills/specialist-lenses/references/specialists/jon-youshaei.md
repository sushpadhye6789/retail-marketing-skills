# Jon Youshaei

**Lens:** A creator partnership should be evaluated and negotiated on actual performance signals (retention, click-through, engagement quality) — not follower count, which is a weak, easily-inflated proxy for whether a creator's audience actually pays attention and acts.

## Public framework

Youshaei (5 years as Head of Creator Product Marketing at YouTube, now an independent creator advisor and TubeBuddy's Head Creator Advisor) teaches creator-side fundamentals that translate directly to how brands should evaluate partners: content performance depends heavily on **hook structure** (the first few seconds determining whether a viewer stays) and **retention rate** (how long viewers actually watch, not just whether they clicked) — metrics that matter far more than follower count for whether a sponsored placement actually gets seen and acted on. His Insider Accelerator program teaches creators to negotiate brand deals based on these performance metrics rather than audience size alone.

## Retail application

Directly useful for `influencer-marketing`'s partner-evaluation and negotiation guidance: a retailer choosing between two creators with similar follower counts should be evaluating retention rate and engagement quality (does the audience actually watch/engage, not just follow), and Youshaei's negotiate-on-performance framing gives a concrete basis for structuring deal terms (performance-based rather than flat follower-count-based pricing) — directly reinforcing this repo's `growth-playbooks` catalog entry noting Gymshark's early creator work prioritized genuine engagement over reach alone.

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Evaluating creator partnership candidates on retention/engagement rather than follower count; structuring performance-based deal terms instead of flat-rate-per-follower pricing.
**Blind spots:** Primarily YouTube/long-form-video-native expertise (his background is specifically YouTube creator product marketing) — the retention/hook-structure principles generalize to short-form platforms, but the specific tactics are most directly applicable to YouTube and long-form content.
