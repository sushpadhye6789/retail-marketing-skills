# Wholesale/Trade Account Prospecting Reference

For when the user sells a physical product wholesale and needs to find independent retailers, boutiques, distributors, or trade accounts to pitch a line to (see `marketing-strategy` Section 16 for the B2B/Wholesale channel context, and `trade-marketing` for the sell-in pitch itself once a target is qualified).

---

## ICP Signals That Matter (Wholesale branch)

Beyond standard firmographics (category, location, store count), a wholesale/trade prospect is qualified by:

### Assortment-fit signals

- **Existing brand mix** — do they already carry adjacent/complementary brands (a genuine fit) or direct competitors (a harder sell, or a real displacement opportunity)?
- **Category gap** — is there a visible hole in their current range (price point, use case, style) this product would fill?
- **Store format and positioning** — does the retailer's own positioning (premium boutique, value/discount, specialty) match the brand's tier (see `marketing-strategy/references/brand-tier-guide.md`)?

### Growth/readiness signals

- **New store openings or expansion** — a retailer opening new locations has fresh shelf space and buying activity
- **Recent range review or reset announced** — category resets are exactly when a buyer is actively evaluating new lines (see `trade-marketing`'s Category Management & Line Reviews)
- **Active social/press presence featuring new brands** — a retailer that visibly promotes new arrivals is more receptive to pitches than one that hasn't changed its assortment in years
- **Attendance at relevant trade shows** — a retailer or buyer active at the category's trade shows is actively sourcing new lines

### Decay signals (downgrade scoring)

- Recent store closures or downsizing
- No new-brand activity visible in the last 12+ months
- A buyer/category manager change with no clarity on the new person's priorities yet (wait for signal, don't guess)
- Financial distress signals (public reporting, industry news of struggles)

## Discovery Sources (Wholesale branch)

Combine 2+ sources for cross-verification.

### Tier 1 — primary discovery

- **Trade show exhibitor/attendee lists** — the highest-intent source; a retailer or buyer attending the category's trade show is actively sourcing
- **Industry trade publications and buyer directories** — category-specific trade press regularly profiles retailers and buyers
- **Competitor's stockist list** — a competitor's own "where to buy" page is a direct list of retailers already carrying a comparable product

### Tier 2 — enrichment and verification

- **Store locator / stockist pages** on adjacent or complementary brands' sites
- **Local business directories and Chamber of Commerce listings** for independent/regional retailers (see `local-marketing`'s citation sources)
- **LinkedIn** for identifying the actual buyer/category manager at a larger chain (a genuinely different research task from finding an independent store's owner, who is often the buyer)

### Tier 3 — direct outreach validation

- **Store visits or mystery-shop research** — for a high-priority target, an in-person visit (or a detailed look at their site/social) confirms assortment fit before outreach, rather than guessing from a directory listing alone

## Qualification Rubric (Wholesale branch)

Score each prospect Hot / Warm / Cold:

| Signal | Hot | Warm | Cold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assortment fit | Clear category gap, no direct competitor carried | Adjacent category, some competitor overlap | Direct competitor already dominant in the category |
| Store/brand tier match | Strong tier alignment | Roughly aligned | Clear mismatch (budget brand pitching a luxury boutique, or vice versa) |
| Buying readiness | Active range review or recent expansion | No specific signal either way | Recent downsizing or no new-brand activity in 12+ months |
| Reachability | Named buyer/category manager identified | General store contact only | No identifiable contact |

## Business (Small/Medium) vs. Commercial (Large) — Different Prospecting Motions

See `marketing-strategy`'s Section 16 Business/Commercial split — it changes how prospecting itself runs, not just the pitch:

- **Business (independent/small retailers)**: higher volume, lower per-account value, often reachable directly by the owner. Prospecting looks more like the **Local SMB** branch's volume-oriented approach — cast a wider net, qualify fast, expect a shorter sales cycle.
- **Commercial (large chains/national accounts)**: lower volume, higher per-account value, requires identifying the actual category manager or buyer (not just "the store"), and a much longer, relationship-driven qualification process — closer to the standard **B2B** branch's named-account research depth.

Don't apply Business-segment volume tactics to a Commercial target, or vice versa — see the Common Mistakes section in the main skill file.

## Output Columns (Wholesale branch)

In addition to the standard columns (name, contact, source, score), include:

- **Store format** (boutique, chain, specialty, department, online-only)
- **Estimated store count** (single location vs. multi-location — determines Business vs. Commercial segment)
- **Current brand mix / category gap identified**
- **Trade show or event where discovered, if applicable**
