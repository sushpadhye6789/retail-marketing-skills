# Joey Coleman

**Lens:** Between 20-70% of newly acquired customers churn within their first 100 days, not because the product is bad, but because they feel neglected immediately after the sale — the moment marketing declares victory is exactly when the real retention work should start.

## Public framework

Coleman's **First 100 Days®** methodology, from his Wall Street Journal bestseller *Never Lose a Customer Again*, structures post-sale customer experience into eight phases (the "8 A's"): **Assess** (understand the customer's actual expectations), **Admit** (acknowledge the decision they just made), **Affirm** (reassure them it was the right call), **Activate** (get them to first use), **Acclimate** (help them get comfortable), **Accomplish** (help them reach a real result), **Adopt** (make the product/brand part of their identity), and **Advocate** (turn them into a promoter). The core claim: implementing structured early-relationship attention drives measurable increases in customer lifetime value and profit.

## Retail application

This extends `onboarding` (currently written with a SaaS/signup-flow lean) and `loyalty`/`churn-prevention` with a retail-appropriate version of the same idea: the period right after a purchase — order confirmation, delivery, unboxing, first use, warranty registration — is under-owned by most retail marketing, which treats "sale complete" as the finish line. A structured post-purchase sequence (a genuine welcome beyond a shipping notification, proactive care/usage guidance before a support question arises, a check-in at the point the customer would naturally start using the product) applies the 8-A logic without needing a SaaS-style "activation event."

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Designing a post-purchase communication sequence for considered or higher-value purchases (durable goods, first purchase from a new brand) where early neglect risk is real; diagnosing early churn or return patterns as a relationship-design problem, not just a product-quality one.
**Blind spots:** The framework assumes an ongoing relationship worth investing 100 days into — for genuinely low-consideration, single-purchase retail categories, a lighter-touch version (a few key moments, not eight formal phases) is more proportionate than the full methodology.
