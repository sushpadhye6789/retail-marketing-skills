# Capital-Stage Capability Unlocks

Every marketing plan must include explicit "what changes when the next round/loan closes / when budget unlocks" reasoning. This makes the plan investor- or owner-friendly and operationally honest.

This doc defines the standard tiers, built around **revenue scale and capital source** rather than the startup-funding-round vocabulary (pre-seed/Series A/etc.) most marketing-planning material assumes — most clients this plan serves are bootstrapped, self-funded, or bank/loan-financed retail and DTC businesses, not venture-backed startups. Use the tiers as anchors, adjust for client category and unit economics.

**Related docs:**
- `budget-planning.md` — two scientific methods for setting the actual budget number (Revenue-Based 2–30% of annual revenue, or Goal-Based reverse-engineered from the revenue target), CAC calculation, experimental buffer
- `growth-patterns.md` — the real shape of retail/DTC growth by phase ($0–250K / $250K–1.5M / $1.5M–10M / $10M+), linear vs step-function, S-curve layering
- `team-and-agency-model.md` — what each tier means for team composition, the first marketing hire, and the in-house vs outsource ratio

## Why capital stage matters in a marketing plan

Most marketing plans are written as if budget is unconstrained. That's a failure mode for early-stage clients — it produces aspirational lists rather than executable roadmaps.

The fix: tie every recommendation to a budget tier. The plan stays honest about what's executable today, and the team / investors / lender see explicitly what each tier of capital (raised, borrowed, or reinvested) unlocks.

This also helps a founder mid-raise or mid-loan-application: showing what the capital buys is investor- or lender-narrative material.

## Standard tiers

### Tier 1 — Bootstrapped / self-funded

**Budget profile:**
- Annual revenue: typically under ~$250K–500K (see `growth-patterns.md` Phase 1)
- Paid acquisition: $0
- Tooling stack: ~$150–600/mo (ecommerce platform fee, a basic email/SMS tool, GA4 free, Canva, payment processing)
- Retainers / fCMO: variable (fractional only)
- Headcount: founder(s) + maybe 1–2 multipurpose hires

**Marketing capability:**
- Organic only — SEO, content, social, founder-led local marketing/experiential (if there's a physical presence), WOM, early referral/ambassador
- Limited PR (founder-led pitches, HARO responses, local press)
- No paid layer

**Channels live:** Organic SEO, content, social, local marketing (if applicable), WOM, referral, founder-led PR

**What an fCMO does:** Strategy + lifecycle (email/SMS) + content + SEO + post-purchase experience + community. Hands-on with the skill library doing the operational lift.

**Hires unlocked:** None. The plan must execute with the current team + agentic stack.

### Tier 2 — First outside capital / early traction

A small-business loan, a friends-and-family round, revenue-based financing, or simply crossing the Phase 1→2 revenue threshold on reinvested profit.

**Budget profile:**
- Paid acquisition: $2–8K/mo test budget
- Tooling stack: $500–1,500/mo (paid ad accounts, a proper ESP if not already in place, basic analytics beyond GA4)
- Retainers / fCMO: continued
- Headcount: + first dedicated marketing hire

**Marketing capability:**
- Above + paid acquisition pilot (Meta, Google, TikTok — see `ads`)
- Begin a structured PR push, often timed to a funding/launch/collection moment
- First major launch or collection-drop marketing push (see `launch`)

**Channels live:** All Tier 1 + paid acquisition (small) + active PR

**Hires unlocked:**
- Lifecycle + content marketing manager (one person doing both, or split)
- OR dedicated growth/performance marketing manager (if heavy paid focus)

**fCMO shifts:** From hands-on to strategy + ops oversight. Hires the dedicated marketer. Sets up the channel playbooks before paid scales.

### Tier 3 — Scaling with capital

Angel/seed-equivalent equity, a larger loan facility, or sustained profitable reinvestment. Revenue typically in the $1M–3M range.

**Budget profile:**
- Paid acquisition: $10–30K/mo
- Tooling stack: $1,500–3,000/mo
- Retainers / fCMO: continued
- Headcount: + designer (potentially fractional)

**Marketing capability:**
- Paid scaling across 2–3 channels
- Brand-aligned creative production (designer enables velocity)
- Lifecycle programs fully live across all flows
- First true content production cadence (weekly cadence sustainable)
- First structured wholesale/trade push, if the business has a B2B channel (see `marketing-strategy` Section 16)

**Channels live:** All previous + paid scaling + structured launch motion + trade/wholesale (if applicable)

**Hires unlocked:**
- Designer (brand, creative, web)
- Second marketing manager (if first was lifecycle, second is content; or vice versa)
- Potentially fractional PR if budget allows

**fCMO shifts:** Hands off lifecycle to dedicated owner. Moves to channel-mix strategy + growth analytics.

### Tier 4 — Growth capital

Institutional growth equity or a venture round, sized for a DTC/retail brand rather than a software company. Revenue typically $3M–15M.

**Budget profile:**
- Paid acquisition: $30–100K/mo
- Tooling stack: $3,000–8,000/mo
- Retainers / fCMO: may transition to permanent CMO
- Headcount: full marketing team forming

**Marketing capability:**
- Paid scales aggressively across all proven channels
- Brand campaigns become possible (see `tentpole-campaign`)
- New-market or international consideration begins
- Wholesale/B2B channel expansion, if applicable
- Sophisticated CAC/payback math and attribution (see `ads/references/payback-period.md`, `attribution`)

**Channels live:** Full marketing surface area

**Hires unlocked:**
- Performance marketing lead
- Content lead
- Designer (permanent)
- Potentially: PR firm, paid agency, new-market/regional growth manager
- This tier is often the moment the fCMO transitions out or becomes an advisor

**fCMO shifts:** Often the moment of transition — to permanent CMO hire, fCMO becomes advisor.

### Tier 5 — Institutional scale

Private-equity-backed, a large loan facility, or sustained scale on reinvested profit. Revenue $15M+.

**Budget profile:**
- Paid acquisition: $100K+/mo
- Tooling stack: $8,000–20,000/mo
- Headcount: 10+ marketing org

**Marketing capability:**
- Brand campaigns at category scale
- PR firm partnerships
- Category-level trade show presence and sponsorships (see `experiential-marketing/references/event-portfolio-and-sponsorship.md`)
- Media/IP acquisition as marketing (acquiring a newsletter, a media property, or a complementary brand)

**Channels live:** Everything available

**Hires unlocked:**
- VP Marketing or CMO
- Brand director
- Growth / performance team (3–5 people)
- Content team (3–5 people)
- Designers (2–3)
- PR director or agency partnership
- Regional/international marketing leads

**fCMO involvement:** Typically out of the company by this point — the original fCMO might still be an advisor.

## How to apply tier logic in a plan

### Section 3 (Current state)
- State the client's current tier explicitly: "Current tier: bootstrapped / self-funded per Tier 1."

### Section 4–8 (AARRR sections)
- Note tier-dependent moves: "Paid layer (Tier 2 unlock — held until the loan/round closes)"
- For Tier 1 plans: every move must be executable at current budget tier OR explicitly flagged as future
- For Tier 2+ plans: moves can assume the tier's capability

### Section 10 (12-month outlook)
- Each quarter names the tier that's active: "Q2 — Months 4–6 (post capital close). Capital state: Tier 2."
- Tier transitions trigger plan recalibration moments

### Section 11 (Marketing operations stack)
- Use the table in `references/ops-stack-mapping.md` capability-unlocks section
- Make it client-specific: "Today (Tier 1): {client's current capability}. After capital close (Tier 2): + {what changes}."

## Adjustments by client category

The standard tiers assume a typical DTC/retail brand. Adjust for category:

### DTC / pure-play ecommerce
- Higher paid acquisition floor relative to revenue — no foot traffic to lean on, so every visit is bought or earned
- Tier 2 often starts effectively at $5–10K/mo paid (otherwise can't get statistically meaningful reads on CAC and payback period)
- Payback period discipline (`ads/references/payback-period.md`) matters more here than almost any other category, since paid spend and inventory cash are both tied up simultaneously

### Omnichannel / brick-and-mortar retail chain
- Lower paid-acquisition floor relative to revenue — foot traffic and repeat local customers carry real weight (see `local-marketing`, `experiential-marketing`)
- Co-op or landlord-funded marketing dollars can offset the marketing budget meaningfully — see `supplier-funded-marketing`
- New-location openings are step-function growth events with their own budget line — see `new-store-launch`

### Wholesale / B2B retail brand
- Lower paid-acquisition floor — trade shows, cold outreach, and industry directories produce signal at modest spend (see `marketing-strategy` Section 16, `prospecting`)
- More weight on trade marketing and account-management budget than paid digital
- Often adds a wholesale account manager or sales hire before a content hire

### Hardware/CPG with a subscription attach (subscribe-and-save)
- The one-time-purchase revenue can partly self-fund the subscription program's marketing
- Blended CAC should track across one-time and subscription customers together, not just the subscription cohort — a channel that looks expensive against subscription revenue alone may be efficient once attach-rate revenue is included

### Retail / wholesale marketplace (multi-vendor)
- Each side has its own funnel (buyer acquisition + seller/vendor acquisition) — budget splits accordingly
- Supply-side (seller/vendor) acquisition often dominates early; demand-side dominates after the marketplace reaches liquidity
- Take-rate economics, not order economics, drive the recurring-revenue-style budget math — see `budget-planning.md`'s Recurring-Revenue Variant

## Tier 1 budget detail (most common starting point)

For Tier 1 clients, the marketing budget breakdown typically looks like:

| Line | Typical monthly |
|---|---|
| Email/SMS platform (Klaviyo, Omnisend, or similar) | $0–150 |
| Ecommerce platform fee (Shopify or similar) | $39–100 |
| Payment processing | ~2.9% + 30¢ per transaction |
| GA4 | Free |
| Design/content tool (Canva or similar) | $0–15 |
| Notion or similar for ops | $0–50 |
| Inventory/ops tooling | $0–100 |
| Ahrefs (or similar SEO tool) | $129–399 |
| Typefully or similar (if social cadence) | $13–39 |
| Dub.co or similar (if ambassador/referral tracking) | $0–39 |
| Misc software | $100–300 |
| **Tooling total** | **~$300–1,000/mo** |
| Paid acquisition | $0 |
| fCMO retainer | Variable |

For the plan, this becomes: "Current monthly marketing budget: $X (tooling only, no paid)."

## When to surface tier limits to the founder

If a founder asks for moves that require a future tier:
- Name the requirement: "This is a Tier 2 move (requires $5K+/mo paid budget). Will unlock after the loan/round closes per the 12-month outlook in §10."
- Don't refuse — frame the timing

If a founder underestimates what's needed:
- Be honest: "To scale paid acquisition meaningfully, expect Tier 2 budget. Tier 1 can validate organic; Tier 2 validates paid."

If a founder is over-funded for their stage:
- Don't pad budget to match. Recommend the right work for the funnel state, return excess capacity, suggest investment in compounding assets (content, SEO, loyalty) rather than scaling paid past what the unit economics support.

## Tier-skip cases (worth flagging)

Some businesses skip tiers:
- **A well-known founder or a celebrity/creator-backed launch** — can jump from Tier 1 to Tier 3 directly on the strength of an existing audience
- **A retailer with a signed major wholesale/national-account contract** — can fund pilot deployment from the contract's guaranteed volume, similar to a B2B company funding off a signed enterprise deal
- **A brand with a genuine viral or press moment** — can deploy at Tier 3 levels temporarily even without Tier 3's steady-state revenue, though this needs a clear plan for what happens when the moment fades

If the client is in a tier-skip situation, name it explicitly in the plan rather than forcing them into the standard ladder.
