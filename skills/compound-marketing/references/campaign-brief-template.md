# Campaign Brief Template

A copy-paste template for the Brief stage of any campaign running across more than one channel. This is the single artifact every channel's Draft stage reads from — without it, each channel invents its own version of the message, offer, and featured products, and they drift apart by launch day.

---

## Blank template (copy this)

```markdown
### <campaign name> — Campaign Brief
- **Dates**: <start> to <end>
- **Channels running**: <website / POS / ads / eDM / social / other — name each one, don't leave it implicit>
- **The message**: <the one core claim/hook, traced to marketing-strategy Section 5/6 — not a generic summary, the actual line or angle>
- **The offer**: <terms, depth, dates, exclusions — or "no offer, brand-only campaign" if that's deliberate>
- **Featured products**: <the specific SKUs this campaign leads with — every channel features these, not a channel-specific pick>
- **Visual direction**: <pointer to brand-guidelines, plus anything campaign-specific layered on top — a seasonal palette, a limited-time motif>
- **What's explicitly NOT part of this campaign**: <scope boundary — what a channel owner might assume is included but isn't>
```

---

## Fill-in prompts (answer these, in order)

1. **What's the one thing every channel needs to say the same way?** If you can't compress it to one line, the message isn't sharp enough yet — go back to `marketing-strategy` before drafting anything. → *The message*
2. **Is there a commercial offer, and does every channel have the exact same terms?** A discount end date that's "end of month" on the website and "while stocks last" on a POS sign is already inconsistent — pin it down to one specific answer. → *The offer*
3. **Which SKUs is this campaign actually about?** Name them specifically — "our bestsellers" isn't a featured-products list, it's a placeholder that lets every channel pick differently. → *Featured products*
4. **Which channels are actually running this campaign?** Don't assume — a physical retailer's website team and store team might not default to including each other. Explicitly list every channel, including POS/in-store if there's a physical footprint. → *Channels running*

If you can't answer 1-3 with a specific, single answer (not a range of options), the brief isn't ready to hand to channel teams yet.

## Worked example

```markdown
### Spring Clearance Weekend — Campaign Brief
- **Dates**: March 14–16
- **Channels running**: website, in-store POS/signage, email, SMS, paid social (Meta)
- **The message**: "Last chance on last season — up to 40% off, this weekend only." Ties to positioning's value-for-money proof point, not a generic "sale" claim.
- **The offer**: Up to 40% off marked clearance items only (not full-price stock). Ends 11:59pm March 16, in-store and online. No stacking with loyalty points redemption.
- **Featured products**: The 12 SKUs already flagged in the overstock review (see `.agents/marketing-learnings.md` entry from the last clearance cycle) — website homepage banner, email hero, and in-store end-cap all lead with the same 12, not a store-manager's local pick.
- **Visual direction**: Standard brand-guidelines palette, no seasonal override — this is a commercial moment, not a brand moment, so the visual identity stays business-as-usual rather than introducing a special "sale" aesthetic that could read as discount-era for a mid-market brand.
- **What's explicitly NOT part of this campaign**: New-season stock is not discounted and should not appear in any clearance creative — a common mistake is a store team pulling in a popular new item "since it's already at the register."
```

Notice what makes this usable: every field has one specific answer, not a range — a channel owner reading this brief has nothing left to guess or improvise.

---

## Using the brief

- **Every channel's Draft stage reads this before producing anything** — see `compound-marketing`'s Multi-Channel Campaigns section.
- **Run the Cross-Channel Consistency Check against it before Execute** — verify the message, offer terms, featured products, and visual direction actually match across every channel's draft, not just that each draft individually looks fine.
- **Keep it to one page.** If the brief is getting long enough to need its own table of contents, it's turning into a full marketing plan (see `marketing-plan`) rather than a campaign brief — that's a different document with a different job.
