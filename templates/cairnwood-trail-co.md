# Industry-Specific Template Package: Outdoor/Adventure Gear — Community & Partnerships

**Fictional company throughout: Cairnwood Trail Co.**, a DTC hiking/trail-running gear brand. This package demonstrates the two newest taxonomies from this session with zero private-tier depth until now: `community-models.md` (`community-marketing` skill) and `partnership-types.md` (`co-marketing` skill) — chosen together because outdoor gear is one of the strongest-fit categories for both, and because a real campaign shows them intersecting rather than living in separate silos.

## Why This Vertical Fits Both Taxonomies

Outdoor gear has a real technical learning curve (fit, layering, trail conditions), a lifestyle/identity bigger than any one brand (hiking and trail running are activities people do regardless of what gear they own), and a natural set of local/cause/trade partners (guide services, conservation nonprofits, specialty retailers) that a coffee or candle brand simply doesn't have in the same way. It's a good stress test of both frameworks precisely because more than one model/type plausibly applies — which is the actual judgment call both taxonomies are built to force.

## Package Contents

### 1. Marketing Strategy Foundation

```markdown
### 3.1 Primary Target Audience
Intermediate hikers and trail runners — past the "just bought my first pair of boots" stage, not yet ultra-distance/expedition-level; buy gear based on trail-tested recommendations more than brand advertising.

### 3.2 Persona 1: The Weekend Regular
- Behavioral: Hikes/runs trails most weekends, has a home trail system they know well, values gear that holds up over a full season rather than the newest release
- Key phrase from research: "I want to know it'll actually hold up on the trail I run every Saturday, not just look good in a photo"
```

### 2. Community Model Selection — Worked Example

Applying `community-models.md`'s "Choosing a model" question (primary community goal) to Cairnwood:

**Primary model: Category/Lifestyle.** The goal is broader brand-building and word-of-mouth, not narrowly retention or support deflection — and hiking/trail running is bigger than Cairnwood's product line, exactly the "would still make sense with the brand name removed" test the model requires. The community should function like a trail club that happens to sell gear, not a product-announcement channel.

**Secondary model: Peer-support**, layered on top — gear fit, layering-for-conditions, and trail-specific questions are real ongoing needs (durable goods with a genuine learning curve, per the model's fit criteria), and a Discourse-style Q&A section within the broader Category/Lifestyle community serves that without needing to be its own separate initiative.

**Deliberately not primary: VIP/Loyalty**, even though Cairnwood has a loyalty program. The loyalty program's tiering is a retention mechanic, not the community's organizing identity — conflating the two would mean gating trail-club content behind a spend threshold, which contradicts the whole "identity, not transaction" premise of the Category/Lifestyle model. The loyalty program stays a separate system that occasionally surfaces inside the community (early access to a new trail-tested product for top-tier members), not the reason the community exists.

**Worked structure:**
- **Platform**: a hybrid — a public Discourse-style forum (SEO-rich, good for the Peer-support layer's searchability) plus a smaller, more active Discord for real-time trail-condition sharing and event coordination
- **Core loop**: members post trail reports (conditions, what they wore/used) after a hike — genuinely useful peer content that also naturally surfaces product context without being a sales channel
- **Recurring ritual**: a monthly "first Saturday" group trail meetup, rotating regions, co-hosted with local partners (see Section 4 below — this is where the community and partnership frameworks meet)

### 3. Partnership Type Selection — Worked Examples

Applying `partnership-types.md`'s taxonomy, four types worked through for Cairnwood:

**Type 1 — Co-branded product collaboration:** Cairnwood × a fictional trail-running sock/gaiter brand, releasing a limited co-branded gaiter-and-sock set. Evaluation per the type's fit criteria: comparable audience size (both are mid-size specialty brands, not a mismatch), genuine product-development capacity on both sides, real usage overlap (gaiters and Cairnwood's boots are used together on the same trail conditions). **Verdict: strong fit**, worth the production timeline investment.

**Type 4 — Local business cross-promotion:** partnership with a regional guided-hike outfitter — Cairnwood supplies loaner gear for the outfitter's beginner group hikes, the outfitter promotes Cairnwood to their client list. Near-zero formal process needed (the type's own "almost no formal agreement" guidance), and it's the natural venue for the "first Saturday" community meetup ritual above.

**Type 5 — Cause partnership:** a regional trail-conservation nonprofit — Cairnwood commits a percentage of one product line's sales to trail maintenance funding, verified and reported transparently (per the type's warning against vague "a portion of proceeds" claims). Screened first against the type's fit test: Cairnwood's actual customer base demonstrably cares about trail access/conservation (not an opportunistic bolt-on), so this clears the bar rather than reading as opportunistic.

**Type 6 — Wholesale/trade co-marketing:** co-op marketing funds shared with the specialty outdoor retailers who stock Cairnwood — joint local ads timed to each retailer's own regional hiking season, evaluated per-account rather than as one blanket campaign (per the type's own guidance that this is evaluated one account relationship at a time, not at brand level).

### 4. Where Community and Partnerships Actually Intersect

The monthly "first Saturday" trail meetup (Section 2's community ritual) **is simultaneously** the local business cross-promotion activation (Section 3, Type 4) — co-hosted with the guide outfitter, it serves the community's recurring-ritual need and the partnership's low-formality local promotion need in the same event. Similarly, a portion of the cause-partnership's trail-maintenance funding (Type 5) can be earmarked specifically toward the trails the community's own meetups use — turning an abstract cause commitment into something the community directly experiences and can see the result of.

This is the actual lesson for reusing either framework: **don't plan community rituals and partnership activations as two separate workstreams that happen to both exist.** The highest-leverage moves are usually the ones sitting at the intersection.

## What This Demonstrates — Reusing This for Another Retail Client

1. **Run the "primary model" question explicitly, including naming what you're deliberately NOT choosing and why** — the VIP/Loyalty rejection above is as useful a demonstration as the Category/Lifestyle selection itself.
2. **Score each partnership type against its own fit criteria before committing**, not just against "is this a good idea in general" — the co-branded collab and the local cross-promotion needed completely different levels of formality and evaluation rigor.
3. **Look for the intersection between the community ritual calendar and the partnership activation calendar before finalizing either** — planning them together usually surfaces a combined move that's cheaper and more effective than either alone.
