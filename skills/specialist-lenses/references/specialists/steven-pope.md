# Steven Pope

**Lens:** Amazon PPC, organic ranking, and listing quality aren't three separate workstreams — they're one flywheel where paid sales velocity lifts organic Best Seller Rank, which lifts organic visibility, which makes the next dollar of PPC spend more efficient.

## Public framework

Pope (founder of My Amazon Guy, a 500+ person agency managing $1.2B+ annually across 400+ Amazon brands) teaches **the Amazon PPC Flywheel**: running PPC drives paid sales and sales velocity, which raises a listing's Best Seller Rank, which increases organic visibility and ranking, which drives more organic (non-paid) sales — and the cycle compounds weekly as the algorithm accumulates more performance data on the listing. This is distinct from Amazon's own customer-experience flywheel (selection/price/convenience) — Pope's version is specifically the seller-side mechanic connecting paid spend to organic ranking gains.

## Retail application

Directly extends `retail-media` and `product-feed` for any retailer selling on Amazon: this gives a concrete mental model for why PPC spend on Amazon should be evaluated partly on its organic-ranking lift, not just its direct ROAS — a campaign that looks marginal on direct attribution alone may be paying for itself through the organic visibility gain it generates, which pure last-touch ROAS reporting won't show.

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Understanding why sustained Amazon PPC investment on a listing pays compounding dividends beyond the immediate sale; diagnosing why a listing's organic performance stalls when PPC support is paused.
**Blind spots:** Specific to Amazon's ranking algorithm and PPC mechanics — the flywheel logic doesn't transfer directly to other retail media platforms (Walmart Connect, Target Roundel) even though the general "paid spend can lift organic performance" principle might apply differently on each; verify current Amazon-specific mechanics against his agency's up-to-date content, since Amazon's algorithm changes over time.
