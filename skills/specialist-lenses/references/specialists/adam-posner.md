# Adam Posner

**Lens:** A loyalty program has to balance three tensions at once — meaningful to the member, profitable to the business, believed in by the team running it — and most programs fail by over-indexing on only one.

## Public framework

Posner is Founder/CEO of **The Point of Loyalty**, a loyalty and retention strategy consultancy, and author of the long-running **"For Love or Money"** loyalty research study (Australia/New Zealand, now in its 16th edition), which surveys what actually drives member behavior rather than assuming points-and-discounts is the whole answer. His public commentary (podcast interviews, industry panels) repeatedly returns to two ideas: the **"six currencies"** customers actually value in a loyalty relationship (not just discounts), and the **"sea of sameness"** problem — most loyalty programs look identical to competitors' and fail to earn genuine differentiation or member emotional investment.

## Retail application

This extends this repo's `loyalty` skill's existing points/tiers/subscription-commerce coverage with a harder question: does the program actually differentiate from the three other loyalty programs the customer is already in, or is it a "me-too" points scheme? For retail specifically, the "For Love or Money" research is a useful external benchmark when designing or auditing a loyalty program — it's built from real member survey data, not just industry received wisdom.

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Auditing whether a loyalty program design is genuinely differentiated versus a generic points-and-tiers template; balancing member value against program profitability explicitly rather than assuming they're aligned.
**Blind spots:** Research base is Australia/New Zealand-centric — apply directly there, treat as a directional signal (not a guaranteed match) for other markets. I have lower confidence in the exact framework names here than in the other entries in this catalog — treat "six currencies" and "sea of sameness" as accurately-sourced descriptive terms from his public interviews, not verified as his own official trademarked framework titles, and verify against his current public output before quoting them as such.
