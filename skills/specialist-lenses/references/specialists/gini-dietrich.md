# Gini Dietrich

**Lens:** Paid, earned, shared, and owned media aren't four separate strategies to run in parallel — they're one integrated system where each channel makes the others stronger, and treating them separately wastes the compounding effect.

## Public framework

Dietrich (founder of Spin Sucks) created the **PESO Model©** in 2014, integrating **Paid** (advertising), **Earned** (press coverage, PR), **Shared** (social media), and **Owned** (a brand's own content/channels) media into one operating system rather than four separate budgets and teams. Her framing: each channel amplifies the others — earned coverage gets more reach when paid distribution boosts it, owned content becomes press-worthy when it's genuinely substantive, shared amplification extends earned coverage's life. The model has since scaled well beyond her original book into a widely-used, Syracuse University-certified framework used by companies, agencies, and governments across 15+ countries.

## Retail application

Directly useful for `public-relations` and campaign planning generally: a retail product launch or PR moment planned only as "earned media" (pitch journalists, hope for coverage) misses the compounding effect of pairing it with paid amplification of any coverage received, owned content that gives journalists something substantive to reference, and shared/social distribution that extends the story's life beyond the initial press cycle. PESO gives a checklist for whether a campaign is using all four channels together or leaving compounding value on the table.

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Planning a PR or launch moment across all four media types instead of treating earned coverage as a standalone tactic; auditing whether a campaign's paid, earned, shared, and owned efforts reinforce each other or run in silos.
**Blind spots:** A structural/planning framework for *how channels combine*, not a tactical guide for any one of them individually — pairs with this repo's own `public-relations`, `ads`, `social`, and `content-strategy` for the channel-specific execution.
