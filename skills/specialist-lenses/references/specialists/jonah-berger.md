# Jonah Berger

**Lens:** Ideas and products spread not because they're inherently good, but because they're built to be talked about — virality is a design property, not a lucky accident.

## Public framework

Berger (Wharton professor) developed the **STEPPS framework** in his 2013 book *Contagious: Why Things Catch On*, dissecting six factors behind word-of-mouth spread: **Social Currency** (sharing makes the sharer look good, smart, or in-the-know), **Triggers** (environmental cues that bring the idea to mind), **Emotion** (high-arousal emotion — awe, excitement, anger — drives sharing more than low-arousal states), **Public** visibility (observable behavior gets imitated), **Practical Value** (genuinely useful information gets passed along), and **Stories** (information travels wrapped inside narratives, which carry the product/idea forward with it).

## Retail application

Most retail word-of-mouth advice defaults to "make a good product and hope people talk about it." STEPPS gives a checklist for why some products *do* get talked about and others don't: does unboxing the product create a public, observable moment (Public)? Does owning it say something about the buyer (Social Currency)? Is there a story attached beyond the spec sheet (Stories)? This extends `growth-playbooks`' Viral & Referral Loops entry with the mechanism underneath *why* those loops work, and gives `social` and `content-strategy` a diagnostic for evaluating whether a piece of content or a product moment is actually built to spread.

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Diagnosing why a product or campaign isn't generating organic word-of-mouth despite being genuinely good; designing a launch moment, unboxing experience, or piece of content with shareability built in from the start rather than hoped for after the fact.
**Blind spots:** STEPPS explains what makes content spreadable, not what makes it *true to the brand* — forcing an emotional or public hook onto a product that doesn't naturally have one reads as manufactured. Use as a diagnostic lens on existing strengths, not a checklist to force-fit.

## Go deeper

*Contagious* (2013) and Berger's follow-up *The Catalyst* (on removing barriers to change) carry the full framework and extensive case studies beyond this summary.
