# Bozoma Saint John

**Lens:** A brand earns cultural relevance by genuinely understanding what's happening in culture right now and showing up in it authentically — not by scheduling a generic campaign and hoping it lands.

## Public framework

Saint John's public approach (former CMO roles at Uber, Endeavor, Netflix, and PepsiCo/Apple marketing leadership) centers on two repeated themes in her interviews and keynotes: **cultural fluency** — marketers have to be genuinely aware of what audiences are thinking about *right now*, not just planning against a calendar — and **authenticity as a strategic asset**, not just a personal virtue: she credits her success to not editing herself and owning her voice as a storyteller. Her best-known work (reviving Mountain Dew's relevance through Harlem streetball culture, the widely-cited Apple WWDC keynote, Uber's post-crisis rider/driver storytelling) demonstrates the pattern rather than a single named framework: find where a brand can authentically connect to a live cultural moment or community, and commit to it fully rather than a watered-down, brand-safe version.

## Retail application

This is a genuine gap most retail marketing skips in favor of the calendar (seasonal sales, generic tentpole moments): a cultural-moment tie-in only works if it's authentic to a specific community the brand actually has credibility with, not a brand borrowing a trend's aesthetics. For retail specifically, this means the `tentpole-campaign` and `local-marketing` work of picking *which* cultural moments to lean into should start from "where does this brand have real, earned credibility" rather than "what's trending this month."

## Best for / blind spots

**Best for:** Deciding which cultural moments a brand should genuinely commit to versus generically reference; making a campaign or activation feel authored rather than templated.
**Blind spots:** This is an inspirational/experiential lens built from her own high-profile campaigns at large, culturally visible brands — it's not a repeatable step-by-step framework, and translating it to a smaller retail business means finding the brand's own genuine cultural footing, not copying Uber's or Apple's specific plays.
